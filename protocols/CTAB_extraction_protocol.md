
# CTAB-based DNA extraction from intestinal contents and faeces

## 1. Sample requirements

Recommended starting material:

- Faecal or caecal content: 100 mg
  - For small samples (e.g. mouse faeces): 20–40 mg
  - A minimum of 40 mg is advisable.
- Small intestinal digesta: 200 mg

Samples should be stored at -20°C or -80°C until extraction.

## 2. Reagents

- CTAB extraction buffer
- Phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1)
- Chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1)
- PEG-6000 precipitation solution
- Ice-cold 70% (v/v) ethanol
- Ultrapure or RNA-free water
- RNase-free 0.1 mm zircon/silica beads

## 3. Equipment

- TissueLyser or bead beater
- Refrigerated centrifuge
- 2 ml microcentrifuge tubes suitable for mechanical homogenisation
- 1.5 ml and 2 ml microcentrifuge tubes
- Micropipettes and appropriate tips

## 4. DNA extraction procedure

### 4.1. Sample homogenisation and lysis

1. Transfer the sample to a 2 ml microcentrifuge tube suitable for mechanical homogenisation.
2. Add:
   - 0.5 g RNase-free 0.1 mm zircon/silica beads
   - 500 µl CTAB extraction buffer
   - 500 µl phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (25:24:1)

3. Homogenise the sample using one of the following methods:

**Option A: TissueLyser**

- 2 cycles of 1.5 minutes at 22.5 Hz.
- Use cooled blocks and turn the blocks between cycles to ensure even disruption.
- If necessary, more intensive homogenisation (2 × 4 minutes at 30 Hz) may increase DNA yield.

**Option B: Bead beater**

- 6 cycles of 30 seconds at 6,000 rpm.
- Allow 30 seconds between cycles.
- Cool the samples between cycles.


### 4.2. First extraction

1. Centrifuge for 10 minutes at 6000 x g.
2. Transfer 300 µl of the supernatant to a new microcentrifuge tube.
3. Add 250 µl CTAB extraction buffer to the original sample.
4. Homogenise again.
5. Centrifuge for 10 minutes at 6000 x g.
6. Transfer another 300 µl of supernatant to the previously collected fraction.

### 4.3. Chloroform extraction

1. Add 600 µl chloroform:isoamyl alcohol (24:1) to the pooled supernatants.
2. Centrifuge for 10 seconds at 16000 × g.
3. Transfer the upper aqueous phase to a new 2 ml microcentrifuge tube, avoiding the interphase.

### 4.4. DNA precipitation

1. Add 1200 µl PEG-6000 precipitation solution.
2. Incubate for 2 hours at room temperature.
3. Centrifuge for 20 minutes at 13000 × g.

### 4.5. Washing and resuspension

1. Carefully discard the supernatant without disturbing the DNA pellet.
2. Wash the pellet once with 1 ml ice-cold 70% ethanol.
3. Centrifuge for 20 minutes at 13000 × g.
4. Carefully remove the ethanol.
5. Air-dry the pellet until the residual ethanol has evaporated.
6. Resuspend the DNA pellet in 50–100 µl ultrapure water, depending on the starting amount of sample.

TE buffer can be considered as an alternative/superior resuspension solution when long-term DNA stability is a priority.

## 5. DNA quality control and storage

Assess DNA concentration and purity using an appropriate method (e.g. spectrophotometry or fluorometry).

Store extracted DNA at -20°C until further use.

## 6. Safety considerations

- Perform all steps involving phenol:chloroform:isoamyl alcohol and chloroform:isoamyl alcohol in an appropriate chemical fume hood.
- Wear appropriate personal protective equipment.
- Dispose of phenol- and chloroform-containing waste according to institutional chemical waste procedures.
- Follow the relevant safety data sheets and institutional procedures for handling and disposing of all reagents.
